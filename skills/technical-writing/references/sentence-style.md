# Sentence style

Write directly to the reader in the present tense. Use commands for instructions and active voice when the actor matters.

- Put a condition before the action it controls.
- Keep "only", "not", and other limiters next to the word they modify.
- Split sentences that carry separate thoughts or instructions.
- Keep articles and verbs when removing them creates ambiguity.
- Break long noun strings into clauses.
- Make every pronoun point to one obvious noun.
- Repeat the noun when precision matters more than variety.
- Use one verb for one action throughout a procedure.
- Replace filler, hedging, buzzwords, and invented metaphors with facts.
- Never call a task easy, simple, or quick.

Vary sentence length. Short sentences land a point. Longer sentences may carry one fact with its condition or consequence. Read awkward prose aloud and rewrite it.

Use descriptive links, code formatting for symbols, and the repository's convention for UI labels. Preserve exact quotations and required terminology.
